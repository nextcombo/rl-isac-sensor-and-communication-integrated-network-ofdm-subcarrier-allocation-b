# [RL+ISAC] Sensor and Communication Integrated Network OFDM Subcarrier Allocation Based on Deep Reinforcement Learning [with code]

Introduction With the development of B5 G/6 G networks, the perception capability of wireless networks has become a key characteristic for future applications, such as intelligent vehicle networks, smart cities, and smart homes [1]. Integrated Sensors and Communications (ISAC) networks focus on the joint design of embedded sensor capabilities in communication systems [2]. However, the next-generation wireless network's high demand for faster speeds, lower latency, and larger connection capacity necessitates careful design of the coexistence of communication and sensing. Resource scheduling has been envisaged as an important way to manage the coexistence of communication and sensing within ISAC networks [3]. A reasonable resource allocation architecture design can fully utilize the overall performance of communication and perception, improve system efficiency, and reduce hardware costs.
Many previous works have clarified the paradigms of resource management in ISAC networks. Based on whether the resources are orthogonal to each other, resource allocation in ISAC networks can be divided into two categories: orthogonal resource allocation and non-orthogonal resource allocation [2]. Typically, orthogonal resource allocation can be realized in three ways: time division, frequency division, and spatial division. Compared with non-orthogonal resource allocation, orthogonal resource allocation is easier to implement [2].
Frequency division ISAC schedules orthogonal frequency domain resources for sensing and communication. OFDM has been widely used in 4G and 5G networks [4]. OFDM technology carries channel resources and allows further subcarrier allocation to certain services. Therefore, OFDM technology is considered the foundation of frequency division ISAC. In [5], the author proposed an OFDM subcarrier selection problem with minimizing power between sensing and communication. In [6], a power allocation scheme for OFDM subcarriers aimed at maximizing mutual information was proposed. In [7], the author proposed a joint subcarrier and power allocation method to minimize sidelobe ratio while ensuring communication data rate and distance resolution. However, previous work did not consider the case where there are insufficient OFDM subcarriers, meaning that sensing and communication must reuse some subcarriers. In [8], the author derived the performance boundary of spectrum sharing between sensing and communication. In [9], the author proposed a subchannel allocation problem within integrated sensors, communications, and computing networks.
In this paper, we propose an OFDM multi-carrier ISAC system, focusing on the case where there is insufficient subcarriers. We introduce a reinforcement learning-authorized subcarrier allocation algorithm to maximize the system's energy efficiency. The main contributions of this paper are as follows:
1. Research on the issue of subcarrier duplication between perception and communication in the OFDM ISAC system with insufficient subcarriers.
2. Propose a subcarrier allocation method based on proximity strategy optimization to maximize the system's energy efficiency.
3. Study an OFDM ISAC system with multiple communication users and multiple perception targets, which has seldom been emphasized in previous research.
The article includes illustrations.
Conclusion In this study, we establish a time-domain subcarrier allocation problem for the OFDM ISAC network. This paper aims to explore the carrier allocation strategy under the condition that the communication and perception share the same carrier due to insufficient carrier resources. To address this issue, we design a subcarrier allocation algorithm based on proximity strategy optimization. It is worth noting that in this paper, we construct an ISAC BS with a single antenna and do not utilize spatial degrees of freedom to improve system performance. In the future, we will continue to focus on joint beamforming and subcarrier allocation in the OFDM ISAC network.
